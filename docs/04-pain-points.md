# 04 · Pain Points — Personal Industry Scout

Root-cause framing for why the existing alternatives fail the senior operator audience. Each row names the alternative, the surface complaint, and the underlying mechanism.

## 1. Newsletters (Morning Brew, Lex, Generalist, vertical Substacks)

- **Surface complaint**: "I read three of them and never get the names I actually care about."
- **Root cause**: they are *one-to-many*. The economics force them to write the average reader, not the single subscriber. The closer the writer gets to the audience's specific watchlist, the smaller the audience becomes, and the worse the unit economics. So they don't.
- **What the subscriber actually wants**: a brief written *for them*, against *their* watchlist, signed by a person who knows their portfolio.

## 2. Pitchbook · CB Insights · PitchBook Mosaic · Crunchbase Premium

- **Surface complaint**: "I have to do my own synthesis."
- **Root cause**: these are *databases*, not briefings. They optimise for query coverage, which is the opposite of what an operator needs at 06:00 on Monday: a five-item, five-minute summary written in narrative.
- **Cost frame**: $20–40k/year and the operator still pays an associate to summarise.

## 3. Sell-side IB analyst notes (Goldman, Morgan, Jefferies)

- **Surface complaint**: "I'm not a customer of the bank."
- **Root cause**: notes are gated to clients who pay execution dollars; the operator audience for this product is, by definition, mid-cap and below — they don't trade enough to qualify.

## 4. Bloomberg Terminal

- **Surface complaint**: "Five thousand dollars a month and I still need a person to write the brief."
- **Root cause**: Terminal is a primary-source firehose with extraordinary depth. It's a tool for an analyst, not a deliverable for a partner. The operator audience needs the *output* of a Terminal-equipped analyst, not the Terminal.

## 5. ChatGPT / Claude / Perplexity "research" workflows

- **Surface complaint**: "It hallucinates names, gets dates wrong, and never tells me about the regulator letter."
- **Root cause**: LLMs are pattern-completers without an editorial promise. Without a named human accountable for each claim, the operator can't forward the brief without checking everything. That kills the time-savings.
- **Subtext**: the operator audience has explicitly "tried AI" and explicitly does not want a product that leads with "AI-powered."

## 6. The associate the operator already pays

- **Surface complaint**: "He's leaving in March."
- **Root cause**: hiring and retaining a junior research analyst costs $80–120k all-in, plus management overhead. The operator doesn't want a hire — they want the **deliverable** the hire produces.
- **Wedge**: $95–695/mo replaces a fraction of an FTE for a 5-min Monday brief.

## What "winning" looks like for the audience

- **Five minutes** instead of forty-five spent assembling a Sunday brief.
- **A name on the bottom** of every claim — accountability that LLMs and newsletters don't supply.
- **Their watchlist** — competitors, people, regulators, deal sizes — not a generic feed.
- **A phone number that picks up** mid-week when something blows up.
