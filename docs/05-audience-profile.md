# 05 · Audience Profile — Personal Industry Scout

## ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)

A senior operator or capital allocator at a mid-cap or below who currently spends 30–90 minutes assembling a Sunday-night brief on their industry, has a watchlist of competitors, people and regulators they could write down in five minutes, and pays for at least one external research source today.

**Hard qualifiers**

- Title (or equivalent): VP Corp Dev · GM · partner · principal · CIO · CRO · CEO of a sub-$500M business · family-office allocator.
- Spends today: ≥$2k/year on research (Pitchbook seat, premium newsletters, paid Slack groups, etc.).
- Industry exposure: 1–4 industries they care about by name.
- Geography: NA · EU · APAC English-speaking. (The brief is written in English.)

**Soft qualifiers**

- Already pays an internal associate or hires consultants to "summarise" things.
- Reads on a phone at 06:00 — values brevity over depth.
- Pays for at least one thing in stablecoin already (or has a wallet they used in the last year).

## Persona 1 — Marisa, 41, VP Corp Dev, vertical-SaaS holdco (Operator → Partner)

- **Goals**: spot the next add-on acquisition before sell-side knows; brief CEO Monday 09:00 with five things; defend the ICP-by-industry model.
- **Frustrations**: Pitchbook is a database, not a briefing. Three newsletters, none of which name the people in her CRO's territory. She quietly pays an associate to write a Sunday-night memo for her.
- **Channels**: Bloomberg, Slack DMs with the IB analyst class, LinkedIn, paid Substacks she resents needing.
- **Buying trigger**: a peer forwards a Personal Industry Scout brief in DM with one line — "look at the ServiceTitan item, this is the kind of thing we're missing."
- **Tier**: starts on **Operator** ($95), upgrades to **Partner** ($245) the moment she wants the Thursday alert digest.

## Persona 2 — Wei, 53, Principal of a single-family office, Singapore (Concierge)

- **Goals**: track four industries (private credit, AI infra, vertical-SaaS, climate hardware); not get blindsided; never staff four research desks.
- **Frustrations**: every newsletter sells the same story; he wants a name attached to every claim and a phone number that picks up. He has zero tolerance for "AI-generated" content without a human in the loop.
- **Channels**: WhatsApp groups with peers, Bloomberg, Telegram channels of "scouts" he half-trusts.
- **Buying trigger**: gets blindsided by a CFPB letter that costs him a quarter of work. Decides he's hiring an outside scout the same day.
- **Tier**: **Concierge** ($695). The named scout, the daily Telegram alerts, and the weekly call.

## Anti-personas (we deliberately do **not** sell to)

- **Junior researchers** trying to read upstream of their analyst job. The brief is signed and watermarked to a single buyer; sharing across a team violates the agreement.
- **Content marketers** sourcing material for a free newsletter. The brief is not a content well; we won't give a press pass.
- **Generic newsletter subscribers** comparing this to a $7/month Substack. Wrong audience, wrong price, wrong promise. The landing is intentionally hostile to that comparison.
- **Day traders** wanting price-action signals. We do not write trade ideas. The brief is industry intelligence, not equity research.

## Buying committee (when there is one)

- For Operator: it's almost always single-buyer (the operator herself).
- For Partner: occasionally co-signed by a chief of staff or COO; one decision-maker.
- For Concierge: in family-office or PE settings, principal sometimes runs it past CFO/COO; the principal still decides. Multi-seat is sold via custom contract from the desk inbox.

## What changes Marisa's mind

- The hero memo names companies she actually knows.
- The "30-minute call, watchlist signed at the bottom by you" copy answers her real worry about generic content.
- The named-scout/wax-seal frame implies accountability — different from "newsletter."
- Pricing is on the page, not gated. She can decide alone.
