# 08 · Marketing Strategy — Personal Industry Scout

## Positioning (one line)

> A research analyst on your desk, every Monday — five minutes, signed and dated.

## Positioning ladder (so we pick the right reach)

| Layer | Line |
| --- | --- |
| Tagline | A research analyst on your desk, every Monday. |
| Promise | Five minutes of reading. The deals, hires, releases and regulatory shifts in *your* industry. |
| Proof | Signed by a named scout. Watermarked to your subscriber ID. Built against the watchlist you set at intake. |
| Ask | Subscribe in stablecoin · cancel any week. |

## Messaging hierarchy

Three lines a subscriber should walk away with:

1. **Personal.** It's *your* industry, your watchlist, not a feed.
2. **Signed.** A named human writes and signs every claim.
3. **Five minutes.** It saves an associate's worth of Sunday-night work.

Everything written for the brand should ladder back to one of those three.

## Content pillars

- **The brief itself** — the highest-leverage content surface we have. Sample briefs, week-of-X PDFs, "what made the cut this week" peeks.
- **Named scouts** — short interviews with each lead scout (Marsh in NY, Aslan in London, Cheng in Singapore). Trust transfers when the audience can read a face.
- **Industry desk announcements** — "We just stood up a Defense Procurement desk." Each new desk is a content moment.
- **Editorial standards** — public posts about what we do and don't do (no LLM-only briefs, every claim cited, watermark policy). This *is* the differentiation; it's also the content.

## Content cadence

| Surface | Cadence | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sample brief PDFs | 1 per industry, refreshed quarterly | Lead magnet |
| LinkedIn (named scouts) | 2 posts/week per scout, hand-written | Trust + warm DM funnel |
| Twitter / X | 3 posts/week, brand account | Light visibility, not the funnel |
| Newsletter sponsorship placements | 4 per year | Reach the operator audience inside trusted publications |
| Event breakfasts | 6 per year (NY, SF, LDN, SGP, BER, TOR) | Human conversion |

## Anti-marketing

We deliberately do not:

- Run paid Google / Meta. The price points and audience size make ROAS untenable.
- Publish "10 lessons we learned" SEO content. We are not a content well.
- Run a referral program with cash kickbacks. We give a free month for forwarded brief signups; that's it.
- Use "AI-powered" anywhere on the site. Once.
- Add a "founder's note" or "from the team" section. Every voice on the site is signed by a scout.

## Launch beat (week-by-week)

Tied to the 90-day GTM plan in `09-go-to-market.md`. Brief summary here:

- **Week 1**: 12 sample-brief PDFs published. Landing live. Twelve "founding subscriber" DMs to peers in our network.
- **Week 2**: First lead-scout LinkedIn posts. First newsletter sponsorship.
- **Week 4**: First private breakfast (NY). 5 attendees → target 3 conversions.
- **Week 8**: Coverage announcement — "we stood up the [X] desk." Press to industry-relevant Substacks.
- **Week 12**: Subscriber milestone post — "100 subscribers, here's what they're reading." Names redacted, themes named.

## Brand metrics

- Brand search volume for "Personal Industry Scout" + "personal-industry-scout" — month-over-month.
- Forwarded-brief referrals as % of new signups.
- Reply-rate on the hand-DM channel.

## Voice checklist (quick reference)

- [ ] Signed by a name.
- [ ] Dated and source-cited.
- [ ] Fewer adjectives than nouns.
- [ ] No "we believe" — say it or don't.
- [ ] No "actionable insights." No "wartime CEOs." No "AI-powered."
